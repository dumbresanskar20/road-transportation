Rule-Based Expert System for Road Transportation

A classical Artificial Intelligence project showcasing declarative knowledge engineering, forward-chaining deduction, conflict resolution, and transparent explanation facilities.

Problem Statement

Modern road transit networks face unpredictable hazards including sudden weather shifts, heavy vehicular congestion, roadway closures, surface degradation, disabled vehicles, and accidents. Commuters and logistics operators often struggle to synthesize multiple simultaneous road variables into prioritized, safe operational decisions.

This project addresses that challenge by formalizing transport safety heuristics into structured declarative IF–THEN production rules. The forward-chaining inference engine autonomously matches prevailing road facts against the knowledge base, deriving explainable and actionable advisories without reliance on opaque neural networks or paid third-party APIs.

Core Project Objectives
  • Knowledge Representation:
    Formalize road safety domains into modular, extensible JSON-based IF-THEN rules.
  • Forward-Chaining Inference:
    Implement a data-driven forward reasoning engine evaluating conjunctions (AND) and disjunctions (OR).
  • Conflict Resolution & Prioritization:
    Enforce safety hierarchies where emergency and road closure rules supersede routine advisories.
  • Explanation Facility (XAI):
    Trace and expose precise step-by-step logic demonstrating why each recommendation fired.
Fundamental Artificial Intelligence Concepts Demonstrated
Knowledge Representation

Transportation domain expertise is encoded as declarative production rules with explicit premises, logical operators, and consequents, completely decoupled from procedural code.

Forward Chaining

A data-driven reasoning strategy starting from known user facts (working memory) and driving forward through rule premises to assert valid conclusions and recommendations.

Conflict Resolution

When multiple rules activate concurrently, conflict resolution arbitrates output by priority weighting (High > Medium > Low) and deduplication.

Explanation Facility

Addresses the classic expert system requirement for transparency by logging evaluation traces showing each condition clause evaluated to TRUE or FALSE.

Domain Safety Invariants

Ensures contradictory directives cannot co-occur. For example, roads marked as closed can never be recommended as active transit options.

Persistent Working Memory

Every assessment episode is serialized and stored in an ACID-compliant SQLite relational database, enabling longitudinal audit trails and empirical analytics.

System Limitations
  • Static Knowledge Scope: Inference is strictly bounded by the rules configured in the knowledge base; novel situations not covered by rules yield neutral advisories.
  • No Live Sensor Integration: Inputs rely on self-reported facts rather than real-time GPS telemetry, road cameras, or live weather APIs.
  • Non-Probabilistic Reasoning: Uses classical binary logic (True/False) without fuzzy logic certainty factors or Bayesian belief probabilities.
  • Advisory Nature: The platform cannot override official traffic police directives or emergency personnel on scene.
Future Enhancements
  • Certainty Factors (MYCIN Model): Integrate confidence weights [0.0 – 1.0] for uncertain weather forecasts and ambiguous road conditions.
  • Admin Rule Authoring GUI: Enable authorized domain experts to add, modify, or toggle rules directly through a protected web interface.
  • GIS & Map Integration: Incorporate OpenStreetMap or Leaflet to visualize road closure polygons and high-hazard corridors.
  • Backward Chaining Goal Verification: Allow users to test hypotheses (e.g., "Is Route A safe for heavy freight transport?").
